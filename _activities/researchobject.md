Research Object Crate ([RO-Crate](https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/)) is a community-maintained method for packaging data with structured, machine-readable metadata, based on existing Web standards (JSON-LD, Schema.org). Research outputs are distributed as a coherent package that embeds the methods used, data produced, outputs generated and their research context.  RO-Crate has evolved into a shared, international approach applied across disciplines, organisations, and research infrastructures. 

RO-Crate is not an application, but a set of practices for describing research data and its context, formalised through example driven specifications that can be readily implemented by research software. A growing ecosystem of tools and integrations (e.g. libraries & UIs) has enabled global cohesion around a common core metadata model, supporting platform independent exchange and archiving (e.g. Electronic Lab Notebooks). 

The strategic impact of RO-Crate lies in its extensibility. Multiple communities have made RO-Crate profiles, specialised independently following shared principles of simplicity (“minimally viable metadata”) and specificity, encoding domain specific descriptions without sacrificing interoperability. Profiles can be formalised as Profile Crates, shared via registries, and used for automated validation. 
 
Building on Linked Data technologies and an incremental approach to FAIR principles (“just enough semantics”), RO-Crate lifts metadata from platform specific systems into a common open format. RO-Crate supports packaging data together with software and computational context and is adopted by workflow systems and research infrastructures for provenance capture and reproducibility.

The eScience Lab is leading the community efforts of the Research Object implementation, in collaboration with University of Queensland.
