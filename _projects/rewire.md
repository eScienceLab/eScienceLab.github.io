**REWIRE is a Horizon Europe project coordinated by Athena Research Center. Funded by the European Union under Grant agreement ID: 101287431, the project runs from September 2026 to August 2029 and brings together 12 partner organisations from 8 European countries to support the wider adoption of reproducible research practices across the European Research Area.**

Scientific progress depends on the ability to verify, repeat, and build upon previous research. Yet as research becomes increasingly data-intensive, computational, and AI-driven, ensuring that results can be reproduced remains a significant challenge.

To help address this challenge, the Horizon Europe project [REWIRE](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/101287431) (**Reproducibility Enablement for Widespread Implementation, Reuse and Evidence in the European Research Area**) brings together 12 organisations from across Europe to support the wider adoption of reproducible research practices and strengthen trust in research outcomes. Coordinated by **Athena Research Center**, REWIRE aims to help make reproducibility a standard part of the research process rather than an additional burden for researchers.

## Why reproducibility matters

Researchers increasingly rely on complex software, workflows, datasets, computational environments, and AI-driven methods to generate scientific results. While Open Science has significantly improved access to publications and research data, reproducing published findings often remains difficult because essential information about how research was conducted is not always documented, preserved, or shared in a systematic way.

Improving reproducibility helps strengthen confidence in research, supports the reuse of scientific knowledge, and maximises the value of public investment in research and innovation. It also enables researchers, institutions, funders, and publishers to better assess, validate, and build upon existing work.

REWIRE seeks to address these challenges by working with researchers, research-performing organisations, funders, publishers, and research infrastructures across Europe. Through a series of pilot implementations and community-driven activities, the project will identify practical and scalable approaches for embedding reproducibility throughout the research lifecycle.

## From awareness to implementation

Rather than developing entirely new tools, REWIRE focuses on helping organisations adopt and integrate existing, proven approaches that support reproducible research.

The project will work across the stages of planning, conducting, and publishing research, helping organisations document research processes more effectively, preserve research workflows and computational environments, and improve the transparency and reusability of research outputs. REWIRE will also generate practical guidance, implementation pathways, training resources, and evidence-based recommendations that can support broader uptake across the European Research Area.

The project will validate its approach through pilot implementations in diverse research domains, including biodiversity, life sciences, robotics, biomedical artificial intelligence, publishing, and information technology. These pilots will provide real-world experience and evidence on how reproducibility practices can be successfully adopted in different organisational and disciplinary contexts.

## RO-Crate and reproducibility workflows

[RO-Crate](/products/researchobject/) is one of REWIRE's three core technical interventions across the research lifecycle: machine-actionable Reproducibility Management Plans (RMPs) for **planning**, [GNU Guix](https://guix.gnu.org/) reproducible computational environments for **execution**, and RO-Crate for **publishing** reproducible research objects.

Within REWIRE, RO-Crate also provides an integration layer between these stages. RO-Crate profiles will connect research plans, computational environments, workflows, data, outputs and provenance in machine-actionable packages that can be validated and exchanged between research infrastructures and repositories. REWIRE will adapt Process, Workflow Run, Recuperative and machine-actionable DMP RO-Crate profiles and test their use across its pilots.

### eScience Lab involvement

The eScience Lab contributes expertise in [RO-Crate](/products/researchobject/), [WorkflowHub](https://workflowhub.eu/), scientific workflows, provenance and FAIR Digital Objects. In REWIRE, the University of Manchester contributes across all five work packages and leads or co-leads work on RO-Crate profiles, BLUEPRINTS evidence synthesis, and pilot implementation support.
